# 5.4 Building a Forecast
*AAI-501 · Week 5 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** Forecasting follows the **same ML lifecycle** as Week 4,
with two big twists: **never shuffle** the data, and **only use
information you'd really have at forecast time** (**ex ante**).

**Where it fits:** 5.1-5.3 gave plots and models. 5.4 ties them into
a full workflow and shows how to **evaluate honestly.**

---
# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
Time series accuracy is easy to fake. A random train/test split
or peeking at future data gives "99% accuracy" that disappears in
real life.

**Hook:** **no time travel.** The model may only see what the
calendar says is already past.

## 1) The lifecycle (same as Week 4)
**Define -> Collect -> Prepare -> Select -> Train -> Deploy and
maintain.**
- **Define:** what to forecast, **how far ahead,** minimum
  acceptable accuracy.
- **Collect:** history **plus exogenous variables** (economy,
  weather, store openings and closings). Getting clean history is
  often **hard** in practice.
- **Prepare:** clean, remove outliers. **Visualization matters
  even more** (hence lecture 5.1).

## 2) Model selection
**Univariate** = only the series and derived features (moving
averages, residuals). **Multivariate** = add other features
(holidays, promotions, GDP growth).

| Approach | What it is |
|---|---|
| **SARIMAX** | SARIMA + **exogenous** linear terms (X). Example: electricity demand + temperature. |
| **Supervised learning** | trees, SVMs, deep learning, using **lag features** + exogenous features |
| **Deep learning** | **RNNs** (next module), **transformers** |

**Bigger is not better:** classical methods (exponential smoothing,
even a basic ARIMA) can **beat deep learning.** The **M4
forecasting competition:** **exponential smoothing** and the
**theta method** performed competitively against complex ML/DL.

## 3) Features for data prep
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Lag features** | rolling mean, min/max, lag differences, x(t-1) (can be more informative than x(t) or x(t-2)) |
| **Time-based** | day of week, holiday, financial quarter, special events, promotions |
| **Exogenous** | weather, economics, marketing spend, social factors (sentiment, macro) |

## 4) Check the residuals
A good model has residuals that are:
1. **Uncorrelated**
2. **Mean zero**
3. **Constant variance**
4. **Normally distributed**
Bad signs: trend, seasonal pattern, **heteroscedasticity** /
**funnel,** outliers, autocorrelation (see 5.1).

## 5) Train/test splits for time
- **Do not shuffle.** Test data comes **after** training data.
  Mixing years leaks the future into training (**information
  leakage**).
- **Let your goal define the split:** quick evaluation? **One-step**
  (next day/week/month) or **multi-step** (next 6 months)?
- **Knowledge accumulation:** will the forecast always see all
  history (**expanding window**) or only a **sliding window**?
- **Rolling split:** slide the train/test split along time to get
  many evaluations; cap training at a window if needed. For
  multi-step forecasts, keep the **target moving** (e.g. always
  4 quarters ahead).

**Trap:** random splits are a classic reason for "99% accuracy."

## 6) Error metrics
Let e(t) = actual - forecast.
| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MAE** | average of **absolute** errors |
| **RMSE** | square root of the average **squared** error |
| **MAPE** | average of **percentage errors:** p(t) = 100 x e(t) / y(t) (lecturer calls it most common) |
You can **weight** errors (e.g. next month matters more than two
months out).

## 7) Ex ante vs ex post
- **Ex ante:** forecast uses **only information available at the
  forecast origin.** Multi-step: **use your April forecast as an
  input for May** (you don't have real April).
- **Ex post:** uses data the model shouldn't have had. Sometimes
  used for quick analysis but explains most "too-good" accuracy.
- **Allowed future info:** things you **know in advance:** St.
  Patrick's Day date, a planned July promotion.

## 8) Judgmental forecasting (expert opinion)
Used when **no data** exists, or to **adjust** statistical
forecasts. Risk: bias. Do it **systematically:**
1. Set the forecasting task **clearly and concisely** (everyone
   agrees on the outcome).
2. Use a **systematic approach** (no fingers on the scale).
3. **Document** judgment updates and why.
4. **Evaluate** forecasts **systematically and objectively.**
5. **Separate forecasters from users** (users are biased).

**Driver-based forecasting:** forecast the target (revenue, demand,
cost) from **historical trends and drivers** (marketing spend, new
products). **Demand planning** is the most common form.

## Big picture recap
- Same lifecycle, plus exogenous data and heavy visualization.
- **SARIMAX** or supervised models with lag features for
  multivariate.
- Simple can beat complex (M4).
- Split by time, check residuals, use MAE/RMSE/MAPE, stay
  **ex ante.**

# LAYER 2: QUIZ REFERENCE

- **Univariate** vs **multivariate** models.
- **SARIMAX:** "extension of SARIMA; **X = exogenous features**";
  adds linear terms for non-time-series features.
- Supervised learning for forecasting needs **lag features.**
- **M4 forecasting competition:** statistical methods
  (**exponential smoothing, theta method**) competitive.
- Feature sets: **lag, time-based, exogenous.**
- Residual criteria: **uncorrelated, mean zero, constant variance,
  normal.**
- **Do not shuffle.** Test after train. **Rolling** train/test
  splits. Window can be **all history** or **sliding window.**
- Metrics: **MAE, RMSE, MAPE** (p(t) = 100 x e(t)/y(t)).
- **Ex ante** vs **ex post.** Known future info allowed.
- **Judgmental forecasting** rules (5 above).
- **Driver-based forecasting;** demand planning = most common.

*Lecture slip: in the RMSE sentence the lecturer says "square root
of the squared error term." Strictly it's the square root of the
**mean** squared error. "SERIMAX" in the transcript = SARIMAX.*
