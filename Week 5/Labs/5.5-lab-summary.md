# 5.5 Lab: StatsForecast (Nixtla)
*AAI-501 · Week 5 Lab · transcript only (no notebook provided, code not run)*

**One line:** Use the **StatsForecast** package to forecast
**12 months** of milk production with three models in one class:
**SeasonalNaive, AutoETS, AutoARIMA.**

**Hook:** **one engine, many models:** hand `StatsForecast` a list
of models and it fits and forecasts them all.

## Steps (as described)
1. **Install:** `pip install statsforecast` (not in the base Colab
   image).
2. **Imports:** pandas, matplotlib, `StatsForecast` (the "forecasting
   engine" that consolidates the steps), and from the models
   sub-package: `SeasonalNaive`, `AutoETS`, `AutoARIMA`.
3. **Data:** monthly milk production, **1962 to 1976**, shows trend,
   seasonality, noise. The lecturer first says "thousands of
   gallons," then corrects: the graph is **pounds per cow.**
4. **Train/test:** train = history up to the last year; forecast
   the next year.
5. **Models:** `SeasonalNaive(season_length=12)`, `AutoETS(...)`,
   `AutoARIMA(...)` with **12** = months per year (data monthly,
   seasonality yearly).
6. **Engine:** `StatsForecast(models=..., freq=..., n_jobs=-1)`;
   `n_jobs=-1` = **parallelize** across models.
7. **Forecast:** call forecast on the training dataframe with
   `h=12` (12 steps = 1 year).
8. **Plot** results. Forecasts "visually seem to do pretty good."

## Key ideas
- **"Auto"** = parameter optimization (p, d, q etc.) is done for
  you.
- Accuracy metrics are **left as an exercise** (use MAE, RMSE, MAPE
  from 5.4).
- **Double-edged sword:** automation is great **90% of the time**;
  the other 10% you must **read the documentation** because
  decisions are hidden. Same story for **Facebook Prophet.**
