# 4.4 Unsupervised Learning
*AAI-501 · Week 4 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** No labels, no "right answer." You look for
**interesting patterns**, and **you** decide what counts as
different or the same.

**Hook:** Clustering = find the groups. Anomaly detection = find the
odd one out. Dimensionality reduction = find the gist.

## No ground truth
- **Anomaly:** a point far from the rest is an outlier. As it moves
  closer, where does it stop being one? Debatable.
- **Clusters:** separate groups blur into one as they get closer.
  When does a cluster stop being independent? Debatable.
- Unsupervised learning has **no gold standard.** The practitioner
  decides.

**3 main types:** clustering, anomaly detection, dimensionality
reduction.

## 1. Clustering
**Goal:** find interesting **subgroups**, group like with like.
**Applications:**
- **Customer segmentation** (trendy millennials, Gen Z, boomers):
  different offers per segment.
- **Document topic modeling** (technology, entertainment, sports).
- **Social network analysis:** find communities from user
  relationships, then advertise or recommend differently.

### K-means (most common)
1. **Choose K** (number of clusters) up front.
2. **Randomly place K centroids** (cluster centers).
3. **Assign** each point to its **closest** centroid (the boundary
   is the line equidistant between centroids).
4. **Recompute** each centroid as the **average** of its points.
5. **Reassign** and repeat until stable.
- Result: minimizes **within-cluster variation.**

### Choosing K: the elbow method
- Tradeoff: **one cluster per point** explains all variance but is
  meaningless. **One giant cluster** tells you nothing.
- Try many values of K and plot **distortion** (similar to MSE).
- More clusters always lower distortion, but there are
  **diminishing returns.** Pick the **elbow.**
- Lecture example: 1 -> 2 clusters helps a lot, 7 -> 8 doesn't.
  People might say K = 2, 3, or 4. **No single right answer.**

### Hierarchical clustering
- **No need to choose K up front.**
- Start with every point; using a distance (e.g., **Euclidean**),
  **fuse the two most similar** points into one (e.g., average
  location). Repeat until everything is one group.
- Result: a **dendrogram.**
- **Height on the y-axis = dissimilarity** at the fusion. Low
  fusions = very similar. High = very dissimilar.
- **Cutting:** slide a **horizontal line** along the y-axis. One cut
  = 2 clusters, lower = 3, etc.
- **Reading rule:** interpret **up and down, NOT left to right.**
  The left/right order of branches is arbitrary and can be flipped.
  Example: points 9 and 2 look close horizontally, but 9 is closer
  to 8, 5, or 7.

## 2. Anomaly detection
**Goal:** find **outliers**, the **inverse of clustering.**
**Applications:**
- **Fraud detection:** e.g., rapid-fire microtransactions.
- **Sensor monitoring:** e.g., food mixer temperature spikes
  (wear), IoT devices.
- **Healthcare:** an irregular heartbeat on an **EKG.**

### Methods (simple first)
| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Z-score** | Far from the mean (z of 2 or 3) = outlier. Same logic as significance tests |
| **IQR** (interquartile range) | Outside Q1 to Q3 range = abnormal |
| **Moving average + IQR or z-score** | For **time series / sequence data** (stock or sales). Tune the threshold to adjust sensitivity (e.g., 25th to 75th percentile of 10-day averages) |
| **Clustering** | A point that forms its own cluster of one |
| **ML methods** | **Matrix profile, isolation forests** (some unsupervised, some supervised). Use if the basics don't work |
- Statistics are **computationally efficient** and widely used.

## 3. Dimensionality reduction
**Goal:** summarize **high-dimensional** data in fewer dimensions.
(5,000 -> 4 is more useful than 3 -> 2, but 3 -> 2 is the best
visual example.)

### PCA (principal component analysis)
- Finds vectors (**linear combinations** of features) that explain
  the **most variance.**
- **PC1** = the single direction capturing the **most variance.**
- **PC2** = the next most variance, but **orthogonal (perpendicular)**
  to PC1. And so on.
- Keep the first few components = **project** the data onto a
  lower-dimensional plane (3D -> 2D).

**Why use it:**
- **Feature reduction** (avoid **collinearity**, the **curse of
  dimensionality**).
- **Recommender systems** (e.g., Netflix places a customer into a
  genre).
- **Gene expression:** thousands of levels -> a few components
  (e.g., tumor vs normal tissue).
- **Climate patterns:** El Nino, North Atlantic oscillation from
  temperature or pressure data.
