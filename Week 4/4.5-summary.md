# 4.5 The ML Lifecycle
*AAI-501 · Week 4 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** Real ML follows a **6-step cycle.** Most of the
effort and most of the failures are in **defining the problem,
getting the data, and cleaning it,** not in picking the model.

**Hook:** **D-C-P-S-T-D:** **D**efine, **C**ollect, **P**repare,
**S**elect, **T**rain, **D**eploy and maintain. Then loop.

## The 6 steps (a cycle)
1. **Define the problem**
2. **Collect data**
3. **Prepare data**
4. **Select a model**
5. **Train**
6. **Deploy and maintain**
- It's a **cycle:** the deployed model's effects inform the
  problem again (new business needs show up).
- Module 4 covered about **half** (model selection and training).
  **Deployment and maintenance** come in the later **MLOps** course.
- This lecture focuses on **problem definition, data collection,
  data preparation.**

## Step 1: Define the problem
- Practitioners build solutions first. **An unused model is
  worthless**, so do **stakeholder reviews** (external customers or
  internal ones like finance or marketing).
- **3 questions, in order:**
  1. **North star outcome:** what do we want to happen?
  2. **Cost-benefit analysis:** budget, limits, proof-of-concept
     stage?
  3. **What must the model do?** accuracy needed, speed, and
     constraints (response time/**latency, security, privacy**).
- Partner with domain experts. Frame the business problem as
  **partly a data problem.**
- Takes only **5-10% of effort** but saves a lot of waste.

## Step 2: Collect data
- **Does the data relate to the problem?** (global forecast on
  only US sales = trouble.)
- **Right granularity?** (daily predictions from monthly data =
  problem.)
- **Quality vs quantity:** balance perfection with stakeholders'
  speed. Do you have enough for what you **need**, not what you'd
  like?
- **Are the labels correct** (supervised)?
- **Are sampling distributions trustworthy?** (cancer images from
  one gender only = poor model.)
- **Ethical and legal constraints:** compliance laws; GenAI data
  acquisition has been questioned. **"Just because you can collect
  data doesn't mean you should."** Involve legal for sensitive
  data.
- Steps 1 and 2 go **back and forth** (rephrase the problem if you
  lack data).

## Step 3: Prepare data (3 parts)
**Data cleaning -> feature selection -> feature generation.**
(EDA belongs here.)

### 3a. Data cleaning
- **Why:** remove **noise** so the model learns true patterns and
  doesn't overfit spurious noise.
- Can take **up to 80% of project time.** "Garbage in, garbage
  out." GenAI can do a **first pass** (e.g., unstructured ->
  structured), with a **human in the loop.**

**Visualize first:**
| Data | Plot | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuous** | **Histogram** | Outliers, multimodal, high peaks |
| **Categorical** | **Bar chart** | Number of levels, nulls, odd codes like **999**, blanks |
| **Time-dependent** | **Trend plot** (each variable over time) | Sudden shifts |
| **Relationships** | **Scatter plots** (each variable vs others, and vs target) | Inter-variable dependency, obvious target relationships |

**Then statistics:**
- **Continuous:** count, mean, min and max, **median (50th
  percentile)**, standard deviation. Reveals skew, missing
  observations, need to **standardize or normalize** (mean 0, std
  1).
- **Categorical/ordinal:** counts per level, tabulation against a
  categorical target (**imbalanced?** -> **over/under-sampling**).
- Always check the **number of duplicates** (otherwise
  overrepresented).

**Common issues and options:**
| Issue | Ask | Options |
|---|---|---|
| **Missing values** | What does it represent? Does the model need it? | **Imputation**, model-based approaches, drop the observation, fix data processing, or do nothing |
| **Duplicates** | Why? Realistic or a processing error? | Remove, fix processing, or keep if significant |
| **Outliers** | What do they indicate? In one variable or many? Noise or meaningful? | **Normalization** to squish, drop, or do nothing |
| **Imbalanced classes** (e.g., 99% vs 1%, **credit card fraud**) | Why? Target or a feature? Does it reflect **bias in collection?** | **Sampling methods such as SMOTE**, revisit processing, or do nothing |

### 3b. Feature selection (why: 4 reasons)
1. **Curse of dimensionality:** more features -> overfitting,
   spurious correlations. Data covering 10% of a 2D space may cover
   only 5% or 1% in 3D.
2. **Smaller models use fewer resources** (compute cost).
3. **More interpretable** (medical diagnosis, loan processing).
4. **Easier to debug and maintain** in production.

**Methods:** **variance inflation factor (VIF)** for
multicollinearity, **statistical significance** in linear models,
**regularization** (**L1, L2, Dropout, Early Stopping**), and
importance via **SHAP values** or **tree methods.** The right
choice depends on the problem.

### 3c. Feature generation
- **Why:** new features can add signal and **raise the
  signal-to-noise ratio.** Some algorithms (linear regression,
  GLMs) **require** transformed features.
- **5 examples:**
  1. **Aggregation:** 7-day average instead of daily.
  2. **Accumulation:** missed payments in the last year.
  3. **Normalization:** e.g., **log transform.**
  4. **Discretization:** age brackets.
  5. **Binarization:** pass/fail groups.
  - Other ideas mentioned: **rate of change/slope**, **products**
    of features.

## Step 4: Select a model
- **What type of problem?** supervised vs unsupervised,
  regression vs classification.
- **Data considerations:** imbalance, normal vs non-normal
  distribution.
- **Accuracy vs interpretability:** a great deep learning model
  isn't interpretable. Pick linear regression or GLMs if needed.
  Work with stakeholders.
- **Try at least 2 to 3 models.** The one you expected to do worst
  may do best.

## Step 5: Train and evaluate
- Supervised: split features/target -> **train/test split or CV**
  -> train -> **tune hyperparameters** -> loop -> final test.
- Unsupervised: e.g., **number of clusters K**, or **width of the
  moving IQR** for anomaly detection.
- **3 questions:** which **hyperparameters** can you tune? What are
  your **compute/time limits** (7 weeks in this course; a GenAI
  project may just use a foundation model)? **What is "good
  enough?"**
- **Better to be in the ballpark in a week than exact in six
  months.** Speed vs accuracy is a tradeoff set with stakeholders.
- **Choose an evaluation metric stakeholders understand** (MSE,
  R-squared, accuracy, or precision).

## Step 6: Deploy and maintain
- **Understand how the model works in production**, not only how
  you built it. Don't **throw the notebook over the fence** to
  MLOps (they rewrite for performance and scale, and debugging
  gets hard).
- **How often will you evaluate it?** Plan to gather new data to
  compare performance today vs six months ago.
- **Know performance requirements** (speed limits algorithm
  choices and deployment effort).
- Know **how an API is built** and the basics of
  **containerization.**
