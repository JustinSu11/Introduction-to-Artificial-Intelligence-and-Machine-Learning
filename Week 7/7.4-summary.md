# 7.4 Multi-armed Bandits
*AAI-501 · Week 7 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** The **multi-armed bandit** is the simplest RL problem:
pick one of **K options** (ads, drugs, slot machines) with unknown
payouts to maximize total reward. Policies: **softmax, UCB,
Thompson sampling, sliding-window Thompson sampling.**

**Where it fits:** the closing, practical RL lecture. One action,
one reward, no Bellman needed.

---
# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
**Explore** to find the best option, **exploit** it once you
know. Bandits make the math easy: there is usually **one action**
(clicked or not, got better or not), so the reward estimate can be a
**simple average** (observed click-through rate), not Q-learning.

**Hook:** a row of **slot machines** in Vegas, each with a secret
payout.

**Example:** **dynamic ad optimization.** 4 creatives for a new
sports drink; metric = **click-through rate (CTR);** true rates
unknown. (Could add context: time of day, location, user
profile.)

## 1) Softmax policy
Turns Q (or average reward) values into **probabilities that sum to
1,** then **sample** from them.
**Temperature tau** controls exploration:
| tau | Effect |
|---|---|
| **large** | more **uniform** (more exploration) |
| **small** | more **greedy** (less exploration) |
Example Q = [2, 1, 0.1] (verified):
| tau | P(A) | P(B) | P(C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.864 | 0.117 | 0.019 |
| **1** | **0.659** | **0.242** | **0.099** |
| 5 | 0.400 | 0.327 | 0.273 |
Lecture quotes **0.65 / 0.24 / 0.11** for tau = 1 (slightly off).

## 2) UCB (Upper Confidence Bound): "optimism"
**Idea:** up-weight actions you **haven't tried much** (uncertain).
**UCB(a) = mean reward of a + c x sqrt( ln(t) / N(a) )**
- **t** = total steps so far, **N(a)** = times a was chosen.
- Fewer tries (small N) -> **bigger bonus.**
Lecture example, **t = 100:**
| Ad | Mean CTR | Chosen | Bonus | **UCB** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.30 | 50 | **0.43** | 0.729 |
| B | 0.25 | 10 | **0.96** | **1.21** |
(The lecture's bonuses match **c = sqrt(2),** my check.) B wins the
next pull despite a lower average because it's under-explored.

## 3) Thompson sampling
**Idea:** keep a **probability distribution** of each ad's success
rate, **sample** from each, **pick the highest sample.**
- Model each ad with a **Beta distribution:** **alpha = successes + 1,
  beta = failures + 1.**
- Each pull: sample every ad's **posterior,** choose the **max
  sample,** observe the reward, **update that ad's posterior.**
- Randomness comes from sampling, so uncertain ads still get tried.
- **Bonus:** you can start with **priors** (e.g. A/B test results
  from the East Coast to start West Coast serving).

## 4) Sliding-window Thompson sampling (SWTS)
**Problem:** what if **rates change** over time (orange drink
works, then everyone loves blue)?
**Fix:** build the posterior only from the **last W observations;**
drop the oldest as new ones arrive.
| W | Effect |
|---|---|
| **large** | **stable,** slow to adapt |
| **small** | **fast adaptation,** more variance |
Like **rolling means** in time series: a tuning parameter.

**Hook:** a **short memory:** forget old data so you can react to
change.

## 5) Building it (same ML lifecycle)
1. **Define:** how many ads, what metric (maybe tie clicks to
   **sales**).
2. **Collect data:** is the website/store **instrumented**
   correctly?
3. **Prepare:** clean; explore past purchase rates; maybe a
   **prior** for Thompson.
4. **Select policy:** test on **synthetic data** where you know the
   best ad, or deploy several and compare **cumulative CTR.**
5. **Train:** update the policy in **real time,** watch for
   **drift** or weird behavior.
6. **Deploy and maintain:** left for MLOps classes.

## Big picture recap
- Bandit = explore/exploit with **K arms.**
- **Simple averages** can replace Q-learning.
- **Softmax** (temperature), **UCB** (optimism), **Thompson**
  (sample from beliefs), **SWTS** (forget old data).
- Real uses: **ads, clinical trials, recommendations.**

# LAYER 2: QUIZ REFERENCE

- Slot machine = **one-armed bandit;** K machines = multi-armed.
- Goal: **maximize total reward over time;** balance exploration
  and exploitation.
- Applications: **clinical trials, online advertising,
  recommendations** (Netflix, Facebook, digital marketplace).
- Dynamic ad optimization: **4 creatives, click-through rate.**
- Bandits **simplify the math:** one action; reward estimate can be
  the **average**; "policies need whatever estimate of the reward
  you give them."
- Choosing a policy ~ choosing a **supervised learning algorithm**;
  test on **synthetic data** or live.
- **Softmax:** converts values to probabilities summing to 1.
  Example 2, 4.3, 1.2, -3.1 (from 6.2). **tau** = temperature:
  larger -> uniform, smaller -> greedy. Q example **2, 1, 0.1** at
  tau = 1.
- **UCB:** "optimistic," explore uncertain actions;
  mean + **c x sqrt(ln t / N_t(a))**; fewer tries -> larger term.
  Example: A 0.3 (50 times, 0.43), B 0.25 (10 times, 0.96).
- **Thompson sampling:** reward probability distribution;
  **Beta (alpha = successes + 1, beta = failures + 1);**
  **posterior;** pick highest sampled value; update; can use
  **priors.**
- **SWTS:** only past **W** observations; faster adaptation to
  change; large W = stable and slow; small W = fast and variable.
- Lifecycle steps for an RL system (above).

## Answers
1. One action per step; reward = simple average; no Bellman
   equations.
2. Large tau = more exploration (uniform); small = greedy.
3. mean + c x sqrt(ln t / N); the rarely tried one.
4. alpha = successes + 1, beta = failures + 1; sample each, pick the
   max, observe, update that posterior.
5. It adapts faster when distributions drift.
6. Large W: stable, slow; small W: quick, noisy.
7. sqrt(2) x sqrt(ln 100 / 10) = **0.96.**
8. It may never discover a better ad.
9. Old data swamps the new pattern, so it adapts slowly.
