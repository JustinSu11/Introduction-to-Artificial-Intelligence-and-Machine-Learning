# 1.2 A History of AI
*AAI-501 · Week 1 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** AI moves in **boom and bust cycles** ("AI
winters"). "Nothing new under the sun": old ideas return
when data and compute catch up.

## Foundations (before 1950)
- **~700 BC, Homer:** mechanical **tripods** that waited on
  the gods at dinner, made by the god of technology and
  blacksmithing.
- **Ancient China:** a **mechanical man** who walks, talks,
  acts like a real human.
- **1600s, Thomas Hobbes:** human thought = **symbolic
  reasoning.** All thought can be written on paper as
  symbols, i.e., reasoning can be reduced to a **repeatable
  process visible to others** (like modern code). Arguably
  kicked off the field.

### Alan Turing = "godfather of AI" (lecturer's view)
Three contributions:
1. **Church-Turing thesis:** all computation can be carried
   out on a **Turing machine** (a **conceptual computer**;
   real computers didn't exist yet).
   - **Cannot be proven, has not been disproven.**
   - No evidence of functions that machines can't compute.
   - Argues **computation isn't just a metaphor for
     intelligence**: strong evidence that **reasoning is
     computation**, so a computer can do it.
2. **Turing test** (below).
3. Created one of the earliest digital computers, the
   **Automatic Computing Engine (ACE).**

*(Lecturer: Hinton = godfather of deep learning; Turing is
the original godfather.)*

### Turing test
- An **intelligence test judging external behavior** (ties
  to the "act" definitions from 1.1).
- **3 players:** **A = computer, B = human responder,
  C = interrogator.** C asks questions and must decide
  which of A/B is the computer.
- **Pass:** computer convinces C it's human **about as often
  as the human can.**
- **April 2025:** LLMs can pass. A **UC San Diego** study
  (two professors): standard 3-person test with **GPT-4.5**.
  GPT-4.5 was judged the human **73%** of the time, i.e.,
  more convincingly human than actual humans.

### Next
- **1943:** **neural networks** first proposed by
  **McCulloch and Pitts.** Neural nets / deep learning are
  "almost 100 years old."
- **1956 Dartmouth conference:** **birthplace of AI**; term
  **"artificial intelligence" coined** here. Organized by
  experts in CS, information theory, etc. Heavy hitters:
  **Marvin Minsky, John McCarthy, Claude Shannon.** Goal:
  establish AI as a discipline and promote collaboration.
- Then **3 boom-bust cycles over ~70 years.** Lecturer:
  we're in the **4th boom.**

## The three early booms
**Hook: Translate, Toy worlds, Rules.**

### Boom 1: 1950s, machine translation
- Driver: **Cold War**, translate **English <-> Russian.**
- **Georgetown + IBM** experiment: successfully translated
  about **60 Russian sentences** into English. Big funding.
- Worked on basic sentences ("my name is John"), failed on
  **context.** Famous example: *"The spirit is willing, but
  the flesh is weak"* -> to Russian and back ->
  *"The whiskey is strong, but the meat is rotten."* (spirit
  read as whiskey, flesh as meat.)
- **1966:** US government **cut funding** -> **AI winter**
  through most of the 1960s.

### Boom 2: 1970s, microworlds
- Idea: the real world is too complex, so **shrink it, strip
  the noise**, make it manageable.
- Led by **Marvin Minsky:** train AI to understand the world
  as a collection of **5, 10, or 15 blocks** and stack/line
  them up.
- **ELIZA** released at the same time: **world's first AI
  chatbot**, a **Rogerian therapist.** Rogerian therapy =
  **reflect the patient's response back as a question.** It
  shrank psychology to simple call-and-response.
- Problem: small worlds **don't generalize.**
- **1974: Lighthill Report** (UK government): declared an
  **"utter failure of artificial intelligence to achieve
  its objectives"** -> **second AI winter.**

### Boom 3: 1980s, expert systems
- Idea: encode **domain expertise** (medicine, science,
  engineering) = a **large rules-based system.**
- **3 parts:**
  1. **Knowledge base:** collection of **if/then rules**
  2. **Inference engine:** applies **logical reasoning** to
     the rules using **user-provided facts** to derive
     conclusions
  3. **Working memory / fact base:** facts or assertions
     about the problem domain
- **Stanford:** **MYCIN** (infection diagnosis) and
  **Dendral** (chemical assistant for finding molecular
  structures).
- Problems: **brittle, hard to maintain.** A new scientific
  discovery meant **retraining the whole system**: no small
  updates, you threw out the rules.
- High-profile failure: **Japan's Fifth Generation Computer
  Systems** (government staked its credibility on it).
- -> **deep AI winter starting early 1990s.**

| Boom | Era | Idea | Why it fell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1950s | Machine translation | Couldn't handle context. US funding cut 1966 |
| 2 | 1970s | Microworlds (blocks, ELIZA) | Didn't generalize. Lighthill 1974 |
| 3 | 1980s | Expert systems | Brittle, unmaintainable, Fifth Generation failed. Winter 1990s |

## The comeback
- **1990s-early 2000s:** **small datasets** + **canonical
  frequentist statistics** (think **A/B tests**). "Statisticians
  were the original data scientists."
- **2005: Hadoop** = one of the first toolsets for massive
  data -> **big data era.**
- **2006: AWS** (first cloud platform provider). **Google
  and Microsoft Azure** followed. Cloud + big-data tools let
  companies collect and synthesize huge data, setting up the
  deep learning revolution.
- **~2012: DJ Patil coined "data scientist"** ("sexiest job
  of the 21st century"). Companies realized: analyze the data
  you collect to find insights.
- **2012: AlexNet** (deep learning architecture). **Geoffrey
  Hinton** (professor) + grad student **Alex Krizhevsky.**
  Won the **ImageNet** competition (image classification,
  the benchmark of the 2000s-2010s) and **blew away** other
  results with accuracy thought impossible.
- **2014:** less than 2 years later, essentially **all
  computer vision is done by neural networks.**
- **2016: AlphaGo** (Google) beats the reigning **Go** world
  champion. Go is exponentially more complex than chess.
- **2017: transformers** = "the most fundamental advancement
  in the history of deep learning." Led to **BERT** and
  **GPT-3** ("the original generative AI model").
- **2021:** first publicly available generative AI apps:
  **DALL-E** (images) and **GitHub Copilot.**
- **2022: ChatGPT** launches.
