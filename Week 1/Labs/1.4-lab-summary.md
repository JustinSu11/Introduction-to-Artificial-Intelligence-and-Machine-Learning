# Lab 1.4: Staying Current with SCP
*AAI-501 · Week 1 Lab · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** AI moves fast. Beat obsolescence with a
habit: **Search -> Collect -> Process (SCP).**

## The assignment (this week and every week)
- **Weekly literature review:** review **one new
  technology-related article published in the last week.**
  To do that you need a **curated collection of sources**
  to pull from (the "collect" in SCP).
- **Week 1 task:** identify **5 information sources** to
  monitor this course + set up a **workflow** describing how
  you'll **monitor and aggregate** the content from them.
- SCP is a **suggestion.** You can use your own system, but
  **think critically about a sustainable habit** and capture
  it in the assignment.

## Why bother
- Goal: get in the **habit of reviewing scientific
  literature, blog posts, and developments** in AI.
- AI is **vast** (computer vision, manufacturing, medical
  diagnosis) and moves at **light speed**: from **1970s
  microworlds** (blocks and shapes) to **superhuman deep
  learning** in ~50 years.
- Staying current avoids the **trap of obsolescence**, keeps
  you using the latest approaches, and is part of why people
  join the field.
- Lecturer (led data scientists): it's "one of the most
  important skillsets."
- Ad hoc options (YouTube Shorts, Towards Data Science posts,
  LinkedIn, asking ChatGPT) done without a system =
  **wasted time, missed insights, lost motivation.**

## SCP at a glance
| Step | Goal |
|---|---|
| **Search** | Find **high-quality, relevant, impactful** sources |
| **Collect** | Curate: having sources doesn't mean reading all of them |
| **Process** | Read at the **right level**; not everything needs 5 deep reads |

**Hook:** Find it -> Funnel it -> Filter it.

## 1. Search: 4 buckets (lecturer's picks, "your mileage may vary")
| Bucket | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Tech company sites** | **Meta** AI blog; **Netflix Tech Blog** (some of the best infrastructure writing) |
| **Aggregator platforms** (like a Substack for data science/AI, many independent writers) | **Towards Data Science** (on Medium), **KDnuggets** |
| **Personal experts** | **Andrej Karpathy** (generative AI / deep learning leader), **Andrew Gelman** (top authority on statistics) |
| **Video** (not only text) | **3Blue1Brown**, **Google Cloud Tech** (YouTube) |

## 2. Collect: one place for everything
- Biggest drag: visiting many websites/video sites. Want a
  **"one-stop shop."**
- Lecturer uses **RSS feeds + Feedly** (has a mobile app) to
  pipe all sources in and **review headlines.**
- Others: **Notion, Pocket.** Tool doesn't matter.
- **Hook:** one **information lake.**

## 3. Process: 3 levels (**Title, Synopsis, Deep dive**)
| Level | Question | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Is the topic **relevant**? | Most articles stop here. E.g., from Netflix: care about the new **causal inference engine**, probably not **content distribution caching** |
| **Synopsis** | What's the **core idea**? | Like reading a **scientific abstract**; skim. A 10-min article takes **1-2 min**. **LLMs are very good here** |
| **Deep dive** | Read it all | Follow URLs/video links. Only for the vital |

**The funnel:** **10 articles/day -> 3 pass title -> 1 deep
dive.** You read/watch 1 instead of 10 by working at the
right level.

## Using generative AI to turbocharge SCP
Lecturer has used SCP **7-8 years**; GenAI = "the next
frontier." It enables:
- A **wider search net**
- **Summaries** of articles
- **Tutoring:** give it an article, ask questions and
  follow-ups

### Prompt template 1: summarize an article
Fields: **visit a URL and summarize** + **persona** (e.g.,
**chief AI officer** = high-level strategy; **staff engineer**
= different tack) + **format:**
1. **Three-sentence summary**
2. **Strategic insight** for enterprise AI adoption
3. **Questions to ask my AI/data team**
4. **Notable quotes or data points**

### Prompt template 2: find the news
Search for the **top 5 new articles in the past week** on
topics you care about; for each give **title and source,
summary, insight or trend, link.**

### Prompt template 3: competitive intelligence
Give a URL to a **competitor's product release/blog post** and
ask:
- What's being released?
- What does it mean for my competitor?
- What does it mean for **my strategy**? (E.g., if you're at
  **Meta** and **Google** releases a new **Gemini**.)
- What makes it **technically or commercially significant**?

### Caution
**GenAI is a copilot, not an autopilot.** Pass outputs
through a **critical thinking filter** (does it make sense,
does it match expectations?). Don't copy and paste. Treat it
as a **junior analyst** giving a point of view: might be wrong,
but more POVs is better.

## NotebookLM (Google)
- Lecturer's daily tool for understanding/synthesizing AI news;
  "the product with the most substantial impact on my learning
  since grad school ~15 years ago." Tutorial links posted.
- **What it is:** a **local language model with
  retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** focused on **the set
  of sources you give it** = "Gemini focusing on a subset of
  information I give it."
- **Flow:** from the RSS feed, take the **5 articles that made
  it to the synopsis phase**, upload to NotebookLM.
- **Features:**
  - **Overarching summary** of all sources
  - **Ask questions** (e.g., "what tools does Claude Code
    support remotely?" about an article on **remote MCP
    support**)
  - **Audio overview** = a generated **podcast** about the
    sources (lecturer is an **auditory learner**: **45-60 min
    of reading -> ~20 min of listening**)
  - **Study guide** and **briefing doc** (cheat sheet style)
